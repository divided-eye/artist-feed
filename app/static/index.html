    Sites

    A site switched off is gone from the app: no button for it at the top, no column in Tracked Artists, no checks for new posts, and Link Finder doesn't use it. The pages you track there are kept, so switching it back on brings them back. Beatport and Discogs have no feed: they are extra places for Link Finder to look, Beatport for a track to buy and Discogs for releases found nowhere else (often vinyl-only), linking the release's main page. Both start switched off, and both are experimental: they were built without access to the real sites, so their results may be missing or wrong. Check their links before relying on them.

    Link Finder searches

    Tick the sites Link Finder looks on. A track found on one higher in the list is preferred: Bandcamp, then Beatport, then SoundCloud, then YouTube, then Discogs. Beatport and Discogs need switching on above first; Discogs is a last resort, searched only when the track wasn't found elsewhere, and can turn up vinyl-only releases. YouTube is only searched for the artists and labels ticked in the YouTube search column of Tracked Artists (for a label, the uploads of its channel you track there are looked through).

    Discogs only lets signed-in apps search it: make a free personal access token at discogs.com → Settings → Developers → Generate new token, and paste it here, then press Save.

    Your SoundCloud account

    Many SoundCloud profiles share a name. Enter your SoundCloud username and the profiles you follow there are preferred whenever a profile has to be picked by name: by Find missing links in Tracked Artists, and by Link Finder when a track isn't on SoundCloud. The list is read again every 12 hours. Your follows must be public.

    Changes take effect when you press Save.

    Show
    One row per artist. Type in the cells, or paste rows copied from a spreadsheet. Leave a cell empty if the artist isn't on that site; separate several pages in one cell with commas. The Artist cell can hold other names the artist goes by, after commas: the first is shown in the feed and Link Finder matches any of them (put a name that contains a comma in "double quotes"). A YouTube channel goes in the YouTube column; tick YouTube search on the rows Link Finder may look for on YouTube. While the table is filtered, Find missing links searches only the rows shown; Find links in a row's Status searches just that row. A cell you clear stays empty on later runs unless you tick that row's Rescan box.

    Link Finder

    Export results
    Each row needs two things: the artist and the track title. That is all a search uses; any other columns are ignored. Paste rows copied from your spreadsheet below (artist, then title), type one Artist - Title per line, or upload an Excel (.xlsx) or CSV file. A header row is optional. A row with no title can only return the artist's page. Each track is looked for in your feed, your Bandcamp purchases, Bandcamp search and the artist's own Bandcamp page, then on SoundCloud, then (if ticked in Settings) on YouTube; if it isn't found you get the artist's SoundCloud profile or Bandcamp page instead (the remixer's, for an edit, remix, bootleg or flip). Settings chooses which sites are searched. Hard-to-find tracks can take up to a minute each.
    Export
    Every track Link Finder has looked up, newest first, under the tidied form of its name (lower case, no punctuation or describing tags), which is what tracks are matched by. Import your list adds how each artist and title should be written and, if the list has them, links: columns named Artist and Title, plus any link columns; text beside a link, such as "(limited time only)", is kept as a note. A link from your list is used when the lookup has found nothing better; a release the lookup found itself, or a link you set here, comes first.
